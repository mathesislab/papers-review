# Review of jtm-2026-0042

## Round 1

### Summary

The authors reduce a marine ice-sheet margin to two stages, a grounded sheet and a shelf, and use it to map the hysteresis of the grounding line under ocean warming. The model is cheap, the question is relevant, and the results agree with flowline models (e.g. Schoof, 2007). I recommend minor revisions.

### General comments

1. The width of the loop is the main result, but its dependence on the friction exponent is shown for three values only. A sweep over m, with the loop width plotted against m, would make the claim in the abstract stronger.

p1, l10: "the width of the loop depends mainly on the basal friction exponent"

Mainly compared to what? Please name the other parameters that were varied and by how much.

2. The ocean forcing relaxes to its target with a single time scale τ. How sensitive are the crossing times to τ → 0 and τ ≥ 100 years?

### Specific comments

p2, l37: "Between the two folds, three steady states coexist for the same forcing, of which the middle one is unstable."

Please show this on a figure: forcing ΔT on the x axis, grounding-line position on the y axis, the unstable branch dashed.

p2, l43–44: "the grounding line passes the upper fold after 120 to 190 years"

Is this range from the friction law alone? Give the values of m at both ends.

- Units: write the accumulation as m a⁻¹ and the melt rate as m a⁻¹ throughout.
- Eq. (2): the exponent (m + n + 3)/(m + 1) ≈ 4.5 for m = 1/3; say so in the text.

## Round 2

The authors have answered all my comments. The new figure of the loop is clear, and the sweep over m supports the abstract. I recommend publication.
